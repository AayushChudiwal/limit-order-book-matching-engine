## Why this matters

Adds are under half the flow (45.2%), and Cancel+Delete together are
*larger* than Adds (45.9%). A benchmark workload of all-Adds -- the easy,
obvious thing to synthesize -- would exercise a completely different
access pattern than what this venue actually sees: mostly repeated
touch-and-remove at existing price levels, not new-level insertion.
Replace (7.9%) is far from negligible either, and is exactly the message
type most ITCH implementations get wrong (see `OrderReplaceViaItchPattern`
in `tests/test_reduce_and_side_of.cpp`).

exchange-core's own published benchmark mix (9% GTC / 3% IOC / 6% cancel /
82% "move") is a sanity check that real order flow is modify/cancel-heavy,
not add-heavy -- not a target to replicate. It's a synthetic mix for a
different, multi-asset matching engine, not derived from ITCH. This
file's numbers, extracted from real NASDAQ PSX flow, are what Phase 4/5
actually benchmark against.
